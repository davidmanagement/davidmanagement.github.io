The Humility Code — For Builders of Artificial Intelligence

A public letter and code of conduct for founders, researchers, executives, and engineers who shape artificial intelligence (AI).

Drawn especially from Jesus’ teachings in Matthew 5–7 (the Sermon on the Mount) and John 17.

Also available as a Google Doc.

1. Preamble — Why Humility Must Govern AI Power

Artificial intelligence concentrates power: the power to persuade, to classify, to automate judgment, to amplify attention, and to reshape work and trust at scale. Power without humility becomes a danger to the people it claims to serve.

Jesus’ prayer in John 17 asks that his followers be kept in the Father’s name, protected from the evil one, sanctified in the truth, and made one—so that the world may know the Father’s love. The Beatitudes open the Sermon on the Mount by blessing the poor in spirit and the meek—those who know their need of God and hold strength under restraint (Matthew 5:3, 5).

This Code does not pretend that technology can save the world, nor that any company or lab is beyond critique. It does not baptize a brand. It calls builders of AI to a harder measure: that greatness in capability must be yoked to poverty of spirit; that unity without coercion, truth without deception, and love without exploitation are the rightful governors of systems that touch human lives.

Humility here is not self-erasure. It is the discipline of power under love.

This document is a Christian-rooted public witness and invitation. Signatories need not pretend a faith they do not hold; those who sign commit to practice these restraints as living obligations, not as marketing.

2. Foundational Witness

In plain language, the teachings that ground this Code include the Beatitudes; salt and light; the heart-level teachings on anger, lust, and oaths; enemy-love; secret giving, prayer, and fasting; treasure and masters; freedom from anxious haste; “ask, seek, knock” and the golden rule; the narrow way and fruit; the house on the rock; and Jesus’ prayer in John 17 for protection from evil, sanctification in truth, and love made known.

3. The Humility Code

Article 1 — Admit Limits (Poverty of Spirit)

Confess what your systems cannot know, cannot feel, and cannot justly decide. Blessed are the poor in spirit (Matthew 5:3). Do not market omniscience. Name uncertainty, failure modes, and domains where human judgment must remain final.

Article 2 — Hold Power Under Restraint (Meekness)

Treat capability as stewardship, not entitlement. Blessed are the meek (Matthew 5:5). Ship features only when you can explain who bears the risk if you are wrong. Prefer reversible risks over irreversible harm at scale.

Article 3 — Hunger for Righteousness Over Profit

When human dignity conflicts with growth metrics, choose dignity. Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness (Matthew 5:6). Revenue is not righteousness.

Article 4 — Mourn Harm; Practice Mercy

Do not rush past the people your systems injure. Blessed are those who mourn, and the merciful (Matthew 5:4, 7). When harm is found, lament first—then repair.

Article 5 — Keep a Pure Heart (No Deceptive Systems)

Refuse interfaces and narratives that deceive users about what the system is, knows, or intends. Blessed are the pure in heart (Matthew 5:8).

Article 6 — Make Peace; Love Your Opponent

Prefer designs that reduce contempt, polarization, and avoidable harm between people. Blessed are the peacemakers (Matthew 5:9); love your enemies (Matthew 5:44).

Article 7 — Endure Cost for Truth

Tell the truth about risks and limits even when it costs reputation, funding, or speed. Blessed are those persecuted for righteousness’ sake (Matthew 5:10–12).

Article 8 — Be Salt and Light; Do Not Perform Virtue for Applause

Practice ethics in substance, not as branding theater. Matthew 5:13–16; Matthew 6:1.

Article 9 — Do Not Store Up Extractive Treasure

Refuse business models that mine vulnerability, addiction, or dependency as primary fuel. Matthew 6:19–21.

Article 10 — Serve One Master

Do not serve unchecked scale as if it were God. You cannot serve God and mammon (Matthew 6:24).

Article 11 — Apply the Golden Rule to Product Decisions

Ship only what you would accept if you or those you love were the least powerful users of the system. Matthew 7:12.

Article 12 — Ask of God; Seek Wisdom Beyond Your Incentives

Pray for wisdom. Then seek counsel beyond your own lab, company, and incentive structure. Matthew 7:7–8.

Article 13 — Build on Rock, Not Sand; Judge by Fruit

Ground claims in truth, not hype. Evaluate systems by their fruit. Matthew 7:15–20, 24–27.

Article 14 — Protect from Evil; Sanctify Work in Truth; Make Love Known

Shield people from malicious use; set work apart by honesty; let the fruit of your systems be recognizable love. John 17:15, 17, 21–23.

4. Commitments for Signatories

  1. Safety over speed when human dignity, bodily safety, or civil rights are at stake.
  2. Refuse deceptive anthropomorphism.
  3. Invite independent critique.
  4. Prefer transparency about limits.
  5. Refuse systems that prey on the vulnerable.
  6. Maintain human accountability — no “the model decided” evasion.
  7. Correct publicly when wrong, with remediation proportional to harm.
  8. Resource ethics and safety work with real authority.
  9. Respect conscience — do not coerce colleagues to silence concerns.
  10. Measure success partly by whether the least powerful users are safer and more truthfully informed.
  11. Mourn and repair when harm is discovered.
  12. Do not weaponize this Code against rivals as a substitute for reforming your own house.

5. Closing — Receive and Live, Do Not Merely Brand

This Code is not a logo, a compliance checkbox, or a weapon against rivals. It is an invitation to builders: receive humility as the rightful governor of AI power.
